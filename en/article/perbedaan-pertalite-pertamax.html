Pertalite vs Pertamax: RON, Engine Compression, and Which One Fits

Stateline MEDIA Newsroom•October 14, 2026•2 min read

Pertalite vs Pertamax: Pertalite is RON 90 for 9:1–10:1 compression engines, Pertamax is RON 92 for 10:1 and higher. Pertamax is blue, Pertalite green.

Key Facts

  • Pertalite has an octane rating of RON 90, while Pertamax is RON 92. The higher the RON, the more resistant the fuel is to knocking.
  • Pertalite is designed for 9:1 to 10:1 compression engines; Pertamax for 10:1 to 11:1 or higher compression engines.
  • Fluid colors: Pertalite is bright green, Pertamax blueish-green. Official coloring by Pertamina to prevent adulteration.
  • Pertamax contains more complete PERTATEC additives: injector cleaners, anti-rust, and fuel-purity guards.
  • Both are lead-free with maximum sulfur content of 500 ppm.
  • Every vehicle manufacturer lists a minimum RON value in the owner's manual; follow that recommendation.

Octane Difference: RON 90 vs RON 92

The most fundamental difference is the octane number, or Research Octane Number (RON). Pertalite is RON 90, Pertamax RON 92. This figure shows the fuel's ability to withstand pressure in the combustion chamber before burning spontaneously.

The higher the RON, the longer fuel resists burning and the smaller the risk of knocking — early detonation that can damage engine components over time. Modern high-compression, fuel-injected engines generally need at least RON 92 for complete combustion. RON information is always printed on stickers at gas station dispensers and near the vehicle fuel cap.

Matching Engine Compression

The practical rule is simple: match fuel to engine compression ratio. Pertalite suits 9:1 to 10:1 compression engines — generally older or standard-spec motorcycles and cars. Pertamax is recommended for 10:1 to 11:1 or higher compression engines, including turbocharged ones.

Using too-low-octane fuel in a high-compression engine can cause light knocking that gradually erodes performance. Conversely, using higher octane than needed causes no harm — it's just less economical.

Color, Additives, and Emissions

You can tell them apart at a glance by color: Pertalite is bright green, Pertamax blueish-green. Fuel from the refinery is actually clear; Pertamina adds coloring as an official marker and to prevent adulteration and fraud.

Pertamax also wins on additives: its PERTATEC formula contains injector cleaners and anti-rust agents that keep the combustion chamber cleaner. The result is lower exhaust emissions and — despite the higher price — potentially better long-term fuel economy thanks to more complete combustion.

So, Which Should You Choose?

The answer is in your owner's manual. Check the engine's compression ratio and the manufacturer's minimum RON requirement, then follow it. For recent scooters and cars averaging above 10:1 compression, Pertamax is the safe choice. When in doubt, ask your brand's official workshop before deciding.

Don't choose on price alone. Fuel that doesn't match engine specs feels cheaper at the pump but can lead to far more expensive service bills.
