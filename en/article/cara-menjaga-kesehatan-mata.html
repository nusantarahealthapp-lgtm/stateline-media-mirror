How to Protect Your Eyes from Screens: Apply the 20-20-20 Rule

Stateline MEDIA Newsroom•October 14, 2026•3 min read

Hours of screen time trigger digital eye strain with symptoms of tired, dry eyes and blurred vision. Apply the 20-20-20 rule, adjust screen position and lighting, keep eyes moist, and get checked if complaints persist.

Watch Out for Digital Eye Strain Symptoms

Staring at a computer, phone, or tablet screen for hours every day can trigger a cluster of symptoms known as digital eye strain, or computer vision syndrome (CVS). It happens when the eye muscles are forced to keep focusing on near objects for long periods without enough recovery breaks.

Commonly reported symptoms include tired and achy eyes, dry or watery eyes, blurred vision, red and sore eyes, headaches, and neck and shoulder pain. According to the US National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH), about 90 percent of people who spend three or more hours a day at a computer experience these symptoms.

Apply the 20-20-20 Rule

The simplest protection is the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes of screen time, shift your gaze to an object about 20 feet (six meters) away for 20 seconds. This brief pause lets the eye muscles relax after focusing at close range.

This rule is backed by science. An Aston University study monitoring participants' gaze through laptop cameras found that 20-20-20 reminders over two weeks significantly reduced dry-eye complaints and discomfort. Set phone reminders to keep the habit consistent.

Adjust Screen Position and Lighting

Position your computer screen about 50-70 cm from your eyes and slightly below eye level so your neck and shoulders stay relaxed. Adjust screen brightness and contrast to balance with room lighting, and avoid direct light reflections on the screen that force your eyes to work harder.

Evidence on blue light remains limited; clinical trials found no strong proof that blue-light-filtering lenses reduce digital eye strain. Instead, use dark mode at night and keep the room bright enough.

Keep Your Eyes Moist and Nourished

Humans normally blink about 15 times per minute, but the rate can drop by half when focusing on a screen, drying eyes out quickly. Blink consciously, and use over-the-counter artificial tears if needed.

Support eye health from within with foods rich in vitamins A, C, and E as well as omega-3s, such as carrots, green vegetables, fatty fish, and nuts. Getting seven to eight hours of sleep also matters, because eye muscles recover while we sleep.

When to See an Eye Doctor

Get routine eye exams, especially if you work more than six hours a day in front of a screen. See an eye doctor promptly if complaints do not improve even after cutting screen time, such as persistent eye soreness, blurred vision, constant red eyes, or frequent headaches.

The doctor will examine your eyes thoroughly, then provide appropriate treatment, from eye drops and corrective glasses to healthier visual habits. Early detection prevents long-term vision problems.
