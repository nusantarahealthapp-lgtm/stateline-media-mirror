M6.9 Earthquake Strikes Russia's Kamchatka, Residents Evacuate

Stateline MEDIA Newsroom•October 17, 2026•3 min read

A 6.9-magnitude earthquake struck Russia's Kamchatka Peninsula on Friday (16/10/2026). The shaking was felt strongly in the city of Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky, prompting several residents to evacuate their apartments; no tsunami threat was issued.

M6.9 at a Depth of 143 Kilometers

A magnitude 6.9 earthquake struck the Kamchatka Peninsula, Russia, on Friday, October 16, 2026. The United States Geological Survey (USGS) recorded the quake at 03:35 UTC, or 15:35 local time, with its epicenter 76 kilometers northeast of Ozernovskiy.

USGS set the hypocenter depth at 143.2 kilometers — classifying it as a relatively deep intermediate-depth earthquake. The epicenter coordinates are at 52.01 degrees north latitude and 157.25 degrees east longitude. The earthquake was revised from an initial estimate of M6.5 to M6.9 following further analysis by seismologists.

Residents of Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky Evacuate

The shaking was felt strongly as far as the city of Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky, the largest city in Kamchatka with a population of more than 181,000 according to census data. Russian news agency TASS, citing the Kamchatka branch of the geophysical service of the Russian Academy of Sciences, reported that the instrumental intensity reached four to five on the scale in the city.

A number of residents chose to evacuate from their apartments because of the shaking. Inside buildings, hanging lamps swayed violently, doors slammed, and kitchen utensils rattled. According to residents' accounts, the shaking, which occurred at around 15:36 local time, lasted about one minute. The shaking was also reported to have been felt in Vilyuchinsk and Yelizovo with lighter intensity.

No Tsunami Threat

The Pacific Tsunami Warning Center (PTWC) stated there is no tsunami threat from this earthquake. The USGS issued a green alert for the quake, meaning a low likelihood of casualties or economic losses.

The USGS model estimates that approximately 228,000 people are in the shaking zone, with 14,000 people experiencing moderate shaking and 214,000 people experiencing light shaking.

Pacific Ring of Fire region

Kamchatka lies on the Pacific Ring of Fire and is known as one of the most seismically active regions in the world. The area sits on the boundary of the Pacific Plate, which routinely produces major earthquakes and volcanic activity. In the past month alone, the region was shaken by several moderate quakes, including a M4.7 in Vilyuchinsk in early October and a M4.6 in Termal'nyy on October 9, 2026.

This earthquake serves as a reminder of the region's high tectonic activity, though its considerable depth helped soften the impact at the surface.
