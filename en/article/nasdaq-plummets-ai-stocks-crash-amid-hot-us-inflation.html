Nasdaq Plummets, AI Stocks Crash Amid Hot US Inflation

Stateline MEDIA Newsroom•October 16, 2026•3 min read

The Nasdaq plunged 1.3% and the S&P 500 fell 0.5% on Thursday (10/15/2026) after September U.S. inflation came in hotter than expected. AI stocks tumbled: SK Hynix dropped 11%, Samsung lost 9.5%, while Nvidia, Micron, and ASML were also dragged down amid concerns over a Federal Reserve interest rate hike.

Table of Contents

  1. Nasdaq Plunges, Dow Gains
  2. AI Stocks Plunge Across Asia and Europe
  3. Bonds Strengthen, Netflix Mass Layoffs

Key Facts

  • The Nasdaq Composite index plummeted 1.3 percent and the S&P 500 dropped 0.5 percent in Thursday (10/15/2026) trading, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average actually rose 273 points, or 0.5 percent.
  • The sell-off was triggered by hotter-than-expected US inflation data for September: headline inflation at 3.7 percent and core inflation at 2.6 percent, which revived concerns over a US central bank (The Fed) interest rate hike in October.
  • Artificial intelligence (AI)-related technology stocks slumped: SK Hynix plunged 11 percent and Samsung Electronics dropped 9.5 percent in Asia; in the US, Nvidia fell 1.7 percent, Micron Technology 4.4 percent, CoreWeave 5.6 percent, ASML Holding 5.6 percent, and Infineon 7.9 percent.
  • Investors are concerned about the heavy debt companies are taking on to fund AI spending amid the potential for rising borrowing costs. US chip stocks fell for an eighth consecutive session.
  • The yield on 10-year US government bonds briefly surged to 5.33 percent following the release of inflation data, before dropping to 5.077 percent—its lowest level in three weeks—amid a flight to safety.
  • Netflix has cut about 6 percent of its workforce in its largest layoffs since 2022, adding to the negative sentiment in the tech sector.

Nasdaq Plunges, Dow Gains

A sell-off hit U.S. technology stocks in trading on Thursday (15/10/2026). The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite index plunged 1.3 percent, while the S&P 500 weakened by 0.5 percent. In contrast, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 273 points or 0.5 percent.

The primary catalyst for the turbulence was the release of September US inflation data that came in hotter than expected. Headline inflation was recorded at 3.7 percent with core inflation at 2.6 percent. These figures revived speculation that the US central bank (The Fed) could raise interest rates in October, which would increase corporate debt costs.

AI Stocks Plunge Across Asia and Europe

Stocks exposed to the artificial intelligence (AI) frenzy became the biggest casualties. In Asia, South Korean memory chip stocks SK Hynix closed down 11 percent, while Samsung Electronics plummeted 9.5 percent.

In the United States, Nvidia shares fell 1.7 percent in pre-market trading, Micron Technology tumbled 4.4 percent, and CoreWeave plummeted 5.6 percent. Nasdaq 100 futures dropped 1.2 percent, signaling a weak opening. In Europe, shares of Dutch semiconductor equipment maker ASML Holding fell 5.6 percent and German chipmaker Infineon lost 7.9 percent.

Investors are increasingly anxious over the mounting debt companies are taking on to fund AI infrastructure spending. Concerns regarding growth and debt financing sent US chip stocks plunging for an eighth consecutive trading day.

Bonds Strengthen, Netflix Mass Layoffs

Amid the panic, investors scrambled for safe-haven assets. The yield on the 10-year US Treasury note, which had spiked nearly 10 basis points to 5.33 percent following the release of inflation data, subsequently dropped 13 basis points to 5.077 percent, its lowest level in three weeks.

Negative sentiment in the tech sector deepened after Netflix cut about 6 percent of its workforce, the streaming company's largest layoffs since 2022. Analysts note that the market is now re-examining the astronomical valuations of AI stocks amid rising funding costs.
