Complete Basic Immunization for Babies: Schedule and Vaccine Types

Stateline MEDIA Newsroom•October 14, 2026•2 min read

Complete basic immunization protects babies from dangerous diseases from an early age. Indonesia's Health Ministry sets a vaccination schedule starting with hepatitis B at birth through measles-rubella — now expanded with PCV, rotavirus, and HPV vaccines.

Key Facts

  • The Health Ministry's complete basic immunization now includes new vaccines: PCV (pneumococcal), rotavirus, and HPV for girls.
  • Ministry schedule: Hepatitis B (0–24 hours), BCG + Polio (1 month), DPT-HB-Hib + Polio + PCV (2, 3, 4 months), Measles-Rubella (9 months).
  • The BCG vaccine is given once at 0–1 month to protect babies against severe TB.
  • Booster immunizations are given at 18 months (DPT-HB-Hib and second Measles-Rubella doses), plus school-based BIAS immunization.
  • Mild side effects such as low fever and soreness at the injection site are normal antibody-building reactions that fade within 1–3 days.
  • If the schedule is missed, parents can catch up by consulting a community health center or pediatrician.

Why Complete Basic Immunization Matters

Complete basic immunization is the series of vaccinations every baby must receive to guard against dangerous infectious diseases such as TB, polio, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, hepatitis B, pneumonia, rotavirus diarrhea, and measles. The more children vaccinated, the smaller the chance of diseases spreading in the community.

The Health Ministry now lists 14 vaccines in the national immunization program, up from 11. The three newcomers are PCV against pneumococcal pneumonia and meningitis, rotavirus against severe diarrhea, and HPV for girls against cervical cancer.

Baby Immunization Schedule, 0–12 Months

Under the Ministry's schedule, babies receive Hepatitis B (HB0) within 24 hours of birth. At 1 month: BCG and oral polio. At 2, 3, and 4 months: DPT-HB-Hib, polio, PCV, and rotavirus.

At 9 months babies get their first Measles-Rubella dose and second injectable polio (IPV) dose. Japanese Encephalitis is given at 10 months and the third PCV dose at 12 months. The pediatric association (IDAI) schedule aligns on mandatory vaccines, adding recommended ones such as influenza and hepatitis A.

Booster and School Immunization

Protection does not stop at age one. At 18 months children receive booster DPT-HB-Hib and a second Measles-Rubella dose. School-age children get the School Children Immunization Month (BIAS): grade 1 receives Measles-Rubella and DT, grades 2 and 5 receive Td.

Girls aged 9–14 receive two HPV doses for cervical cancer protection. Record every dose in the Maternal and Child Health (KIA) book so none are missed.

Side Effects and Missed Schedules

After immunization, some babies develop mild reactions such as low fever, redness at the injection site, fussiness, or a temporary dip in appetite. According to the CDC, these are common signs the body is building antibodies — not a reason to skip future immunizations.

But if a baby shows severe symptoms such as seizures, extreme swelling, or fever above 39°C, seek medical help immediately. If a schedule is missed, don't panic — consult a health center or pediatrician to arrange a safe catch-up plan.
