Essential Nutrition for Pregnant Women: From Folic Acid to Iron

Stateline MEDIA Newsroom•October 14, 2026•2 min read

During pregnancy, a mother's nutritional needs rise to support fetal growth. Folic acid, iron, calcium, protein, and iodine are among the nutrients that must be met every day.

Key Facts

  • Pregnant women need 400–600 micrograms of folic acid per day to prevent neural tube defects in the fetus.
  • Iron needs during pregnancy reach about 27 mg per day to build red blood cells and prevent anemia.
  • Calcium (1,000 mg) and vitamin D (600 IU) per day support the formation of the fetus's bones and teeth.
  • Protein (71 grams) and iodine (220 micrograms) per day are important for fetal tissue growth and thyroid function.
  • Indonesia's Health Ministry runs a supplementary feeding (PMT) program and iron tablets for pregnant women to prevent malnutrition.
  • Supplement needs differ for every mother-to-be — consult a doctor or midwife before taking pregnancy supplements.

Folic Acid: The Fetus's Early Protector

Folic acid is the most crucial nutrient in early pregnancy. It prevents neural tube defects — abnormalities of the baby's brain and spine that begin forming within the first 28 days after conception, when many women do not yet realize they are pregnant.

Women planning a pregnancy are advised to take 400–800 micrograms of folic acid daily until the third month of gestation. Natural folate comes from leafy greens, legumes, whole-grain cereals, and oranges, but supplements are often still prescribed because they are more easily absorbed by the body.

Iron and Calcium for Mother and Fetus

Iron is needed to build the red blood cells that carry oxygen throughout the mother's body and to the fetus. Iron deficiency causes anemia, leaving pregnant women constantly tired, dizzy, weak, and pale. Iron needs during pregnancy reach about 27 mg per day.

Meanwhile, 1,000 mg of calcium daily is needed by the fetus to build bones and teeth, supported by vitamin D which aids calcium absorption. Calcium sources include milk, yogurt, tofu, tempeh, leafy greens, and sardines. Vitamin D comes from fish, eggs, and morning sunlight.

Protein, Iodine, and DHA

Seventy-one grams of protein per day supports the growth of fetal tissue and muscles, while 220 micrograms of iodine daily is vital for thyroid function and the baby's brain development. Iodine deficiency risks impairing fetal development.

Omega-3 fatty acids (DHA), about 200–300 mg per day, support the development of the fetal brain, nervous system, and vision. DHA sources include oily sea fish such as salmon and sardines.

Tips for Meeting Nutritional Needs During Pregnancy

The key is a varied, balanced diet every day — not doubling food portions. The government also provides supplementary feeding (PMT) for mothers in need, plus iron tablets to prevent anemia.

Attend antenatal checkups regularly so the nutritional status of mother and fetus stays monitored. If unsure about supplements, consult a health worker first — never take high-dose supplements without medical advice.
