Grynspan Leads Sixth UN Chief Straw Poll, No Clear Winner

Stateline MEDIA Newsroom•October 17, 2026•3 min read

The UN Security Council's sixth informal straw poll on Friday (16/10) put Rebeca Grynspan, former vice president of Costa Rica, narrowly ahead in the race to become the 10th UN secretary-general. But two of her five opposing votes came from veto-wielding permanent members, so the race remains wide open — and Grynspan could become the first woman to lead the UN.

Grynspan's Narrow Lead in the Sixth Poll

The United Nations Security Council's sixth informal straw poll on Friday (16/10) placed Rebeca Grynspan as the frontrunner in the race to become the 10th UN secretary-general. Yet the former Costa Rican vice president's lead is far from decisive.

Grynspan won nine "encourage" votes, five "discourage," and one "no opinion." Worrying for her camp, two of the five opposing votes came from the council's permanent members — the US, Russia, China, France and Britain — which hold veto power.

That means Grynspan must still convince at least two of the five veto holders before the council issues a formal recommendation to succeed Antonio Guterres.

Tight Rivals and Color-Coded Ballots

Grynspan's closest rival, Carolyn Rodrigues-Birkett, Guyana's ambassador to the UN, collected eight encourage, six discourage and one neutral vote — also with two P5 opponents. Rafael Grossi, director general of the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA), recorded a near-identical result, only with one fewer permanent-member backer.

For the first time, this round used color-coded ballots showing whether a veto-wielding state opposed a candidate — adding transparency as the decisive stage approaches.

A new secretary-general needs at least nine positive votes from the 15 council members and must receive no "discourage" vote from a permanent member. Since every candidate still faces two or more P5 opponents, the door remains wide open for new candidates.

Grynspan: UN Has Become Too Risk-Averse

In an interview with Reuters, Grynspan said the UN had become "too risk-averse" and agreed with US Ambassador Mike Waltz on the need to fix the organization's bureaucracy. She cited her role in negotiating the 2022 Black Sea Grain Initiative as an example of the quiet diplomacy she would bring to the top job.

Grynspan stressed the race is not over, noting that new candidates can still enter the field at any time.

If she wins, Grynspan would make history as the first woman to lead the UN in its eight decades.

Heavy Challenges Await the Next Chief

The Security Council has under three months to choose Antonio Guterres's successor, whose term ends December 31. Whoever wins faces heavy challenges: raging wars, cuts in US contributions to the UN, and the world body's declining political relevance after Washington withdrew from dozens of its agencies.

Against that existential crisis, the next straw poll will determine whether Grynspan can turn her frontrunner status into an actual victory.
