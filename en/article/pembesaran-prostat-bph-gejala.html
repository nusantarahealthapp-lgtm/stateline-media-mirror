Prostate Enlargement (BPH): Symptoms in Elderly Men

Stateline MEDIA Newsroom•October 14, 2026•3 min read

BPH is a benign prostate enlargement common in elderly men that presses on the urinary tract. Symptoms include difficulty urinating, weak stream, frequent nighttime urination, and incomplete emptying. Treatment includes a healthy lifestyle, prescription medication, and surgery when needed.

Table of Contents

  1. What Prostate Enlargement (BPH) Is
  2. BPH Symptoms in Elderly Men
  3. Dangers of Leaving It Untreated

Key Facts

  • BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia) is a benign, non-cancerous enlargement of the prostate gland, common in men as they age, especially over 60.
  • An enlarged prostate presses on the urethra, obstructing urine flow and causing lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS).
  • Symptoms include difficulty starting urination, weak or interrupted urine stream, straining, frequent urination, waking at night to urinate (nocturia), and a feeling of incomplete emptying.
  • If untreated, BPH can cause urinary tract infections, bladder stones, and kidney damage.
  • BPH symptoms resemble early-stage prostate cancer, so screening and routine checkups matter to tell them apart.
  • Treatment includes lifestyle changes, prescription drugs such as tamsulosin or finasteride, minimally invasive procedures, and surgery.

What Prostate Enlargement (BPH) Is

BPH, or benign prostatic hyperplasia, is a benign enlargement of the prostate gland not caused by cancer. The prostate is a small, walnut-sized gland located below the bladder and surrounding the urethra, producing seminal fluid.

This condition commonly affects elderly men. As men age, prostate cells can grow excessively, enlarging the gland and pressing on the urethra. Urine flow from the bladder becomes obstructed, triggering complaints known as lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS).

BPH Symptoms in Elderly Men

BPH symptoms appear gradually and can worsen over time. Common complaints include difficulty starting urination, needing to strain or wait a long time, a weak or interrupted urine stream, and a feeling that the bladder is not fully empty after urinating.

Patients also tend to urinate more frequently, especially at night, known as nocturia, which can disrupt sleep quality. An urgent need to urinate that is hard to hold back, along with dribbling at the end of urination, is also frequently reported. Though initially mild, persistent complaints can disrupt daily activities and should not be ignored.

Dangers of Leaving It Untreated

Many men consider a weakening urine stream or waking at night to urinate as a normal part of aging. As a result, patients often only see a doctor once complications have set in, sometimes when they cannot urinate at all.

If untreated, BPH can cause recurrent urinary tract infections, bladder stones, and kidney damage from back pressure of a full bladder. Remember, BPH is benign, but its symptoms resemble early-stage prostate cancer. That is why early detection through routine screening is vital to tell the two apart.

Treatment and When to See a Doctor

For mild symptoms, doctors generally recommend lifestyle changes such as losing weight, exercising regularly, and cutting caffeine intake. For more bothersome symptoms, doctors may prescribe drugs like tamsulosin, which relaxes prostate and bladder muscles, or finasteride and dutasteride, which help shrink the prostate.

If medication is not enough, minimally invasive procedures up to surgical removal of part of the prostate are available. See a urologist promptly if urinary complaints persist, worsen, or come with pain, blood in urine, or fever. Early treatment keeps quality of life high in old age.
