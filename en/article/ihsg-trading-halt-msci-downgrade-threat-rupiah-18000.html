IHSG Trading Halted After 8% Plunge; MSCI Downgrade Threat, Rupiah Breaches Rp18,000

Stateline MEDIA Newsroom•October 16, 2026•3 min read

The IHSG opened 8.01% lower at 5,393 on Friday (10/16/2026), prompting the Indonesia Stock Exchange to impose a trading halt. The trigger was MSCI's consultation to downgrade Indonesia's equity market from Emerging Market to Standalone Market; the rupiah breached Rp18,063 per U.S. dollar.

Key Facts

  • The IHSG opened 8.01% lower at 5,393.92 on Friday (10/16/2026); 14 stocks rose, 595 fell, 354 were flat; BEI market capitalization at Rp9,538.22 trillion.
  • BEI imposed a 30-minute trading halt at 08:58:00 JATS; trading resumed at 09:28:00 under Regulation II-A and Board Decision Kep-00002/BEI/04-2025.
  • Blue chips hit lower auto-rejection: BBRI -14.84% to Rp2,640, BMRI -14.82% to Rp3,390, TLKM -14.80% to Rp1,900; BREN -14.91%, BRPT -14.79%, CUAN -14.29%, PTRO -14.80%, TPIA -14.84%.
  • MSCI opened a consultation to reclassify Indonesia from Emerging Market to Standalone Market, citing market dysfunction, weak enforcement, and ownership transparency issues; final decision due by June 30, 2027.
  • MSCI maintains its freeze on Indonesia index changes: FIF and NOS adjustments, additions to the IMI, and upgrades to larger-cap segments.
  • Offshore rupiah (NDF) breached Rp18,064/US$ at 07:15 WIB; spot rupiah breached Rp18,063/US$ at 09:10 WIB.
  • Foreign investors recorded net equity selling of US$212.5 million in October 2026 and US$4.87 billion year-to-date; Bank Indonesia pledged continued stabilization.

IHSG Crashes 8.01% Right at the Open

Indonesia's benchmark Jakarta Composite Index (IHSG) opened 8.01 percent lower at 5,393.92 on Friday (10/16/2026), triggering an automatic trading halt by the Indonesia Stock Exchange (BEI). RTI Infokom data showed only 14 stocks rising, while 595 fell and 354 were flat. The exchange's market capitalization stood at Rp9,538.22 trillion.

The trading halt was imposed at 08:58:00 Jakarta Automated Trading System (JATS) time, with trading resuming at 09:28:00. The move follows BEI Regulation II-A and Board Decision Kep-00002/BEI/04-2025: a 30-minute halt when the IHSG drops more than 8 percent, another halt beyond 15 percent, and a trading suspend once losses exceed 20 percent.

Blue-chip stocks hit their lower auto-rejection limits immediately, including Bank Rakyat Indonesia (BBRI), down 14.84 percent to Rp2,640; Bank Mandiri (BMRI), down 14.82 percent to Rp3,390; and Telkom Indonesia (TLKM), down 14.80 percent to Rp1,900. Conglomerate-linked names also collapsed: BREN sank 14.91 percent, BRPT fell 14.79 percent, CUAN dropped 14.29 percent, PTRO slid 14.80 percent, and TPIA tumbled 14.84 percent.

MSCI Opens Downgrade Consultation on Indonesia

The heavy selling followed Morgan Stanley Capital International's (MSCI) announcement on Thursday (10/15/2026) that it had opened a public consultation on reclassifying Indonesia's equity market from Emerging Market to Standalone Market — a status even lower than Frontier Market, comparable to markets such as Argentina.

MSCI cited concerns over market dysfunction, a lack of evidence of effective enforcement, issues around transparency of share ownership, and suspected coordinated trading practices. While the consultation is underway, MSCI is maintaining its freeze on several Indonesia index changes, including Foreign Inclusion Factor (FIF) adjustments, Number of Shares (NOS) changes, additions to the MSCI Indonesia Investable Market Index (IMI), and upgrades to larger-cap segments.

The consultation runs through April 30, 2027, with a final decision due no later than June 30, 2027. BEI President Director Jeffrey Hendrik said the exchange would seek clarification from MSCI and that BEI, together with the Financial Services Authority (OJK), has taken concrete steps to improve the transparency, integrity, and quality of Indonesia's capital market.

Rupiah Breaches Rp18,000 as Foreign Capital Flees

The selloff also hit the rupiah. The offshore rupiah (NDF) breached Rp18,064 per U.S. dollar at 07:15 WIB on Friday, according to Bloomberg data cited by Bloomberg Technoz. On the spot market, the rupiah opened weaker at Rp17,984, then slid 0.61 percent to breach Rp18,063 per dollar by 09:10 WIB.

The rupiah's weakness was driven by negative sentiment from the MSCI announcement, compounded by expectations of another U.S. Federal Reserve rate hike and world oil prices holding near US$105 per barrel. Bank Indonesia monetary management head Erwin G. Hutapea said the central bank continues to take stabilization measures to keep the rupiah moving in line with its fundamentals.

Foreign capital keeps flowing out. Foreign investors recorded net selling of US$212.5 million in equities so far in October 2026, and a cumulative US$4.87 billion since the start of the year. Friday's crash deepens Thursday's correction, when the IHSG closed 1.33 percent lower at 5,863.78.
