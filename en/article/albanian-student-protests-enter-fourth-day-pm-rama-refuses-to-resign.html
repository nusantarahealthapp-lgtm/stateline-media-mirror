Albanian Student Protests Enter Fourth Day, PM Rama Refuses to Resign

Stateline MEDIA Newsroom•October 16, 2026•3 min read

Thousands of students demonstrated in Tirana on the fourth day of anti-government protests in Albania demanding PM Edi Rama's resignation. Rama refused to step down, insisting governments can only be changed through democratic elections.

Key Facts

  • Thousands of Albanians, mostly young people, demonstrated in the capital Tirana on Friday (10/16/2026) — the fourth day of student-led protests demanding Prime Minister Edi Rama's resignation.
  • The demonstrations turned into riots in the capital on Thursday, when protesters smashed windows, raided a bank, and clashed with police. Protests also spread to Lezha, Elbasan, Skhodra, and Korca.
  • Students accuse officials of corruption and neglecting education, and are angry over plans for a luxury resort on Albania's pristine southern coast backed by US President Donald Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner.
  • Prime Minister Edi Rama said he will not resign: governments are changed through democratic elections, not revolutions. He said he is ready to discuss rising education costs but rejected violence.
  • Rama won a fourth term last year on a promise to bring Albania into the European Union, targeting membership as early as 2028. Albania, alongside Montenegro, now leads the accession process among Balkan countries.

Fourth Day of Protests in Tirana

Thousands of Albanians, mostly young people, demonstrated in the capital Tirana on Friday (10/16/2026). It was the fourth day of a student-led protest campaign aimed at forcing Prime Minister Edi Rama from office.

The demonstrations, which are spreading across the country, turned into riots in the capital on Thursday. Protesters smashed windows and traffic lights, raided a bank, and clashed with police. The protests also spread to the coastal town of Lezha, where police clashed with stone-hurling protesters, as well as Elbasan, Skhodra, and Korca.

The students, many wearing masks, faced off against lines of riot police in front of the Education Ministry building. Some small businesses pulled down their shutters and posted notes on their facades expressing support for the demonstrators while asking them not to damage their shops.

Rama: 'We Will Not Bow to Violence'

Prime Minister Edi Rama said he will not resign over the protesters' demands. "Governments are changed through democratic elections. We had elections last year, not a revolution," Rama said in written responses to Reuters on Thursday (10/15).

Rama said he is ready to discuss the rising cost of education, one of the students' demands, but stressed that attacking institutions, destroying public property, and threatening public safety have nothing to do with the students' legitimate demands. On Facebook he wrote, "We will not bow to violence."

Rama won his fourth term last year on a promise to bring Albania into the European Union. He is targeting membership as early as 2028, and alongside Montenegro, Albania now leads the accession process among Balkan countries.

Roots of the Protests: Corruption and the Kushner Resort

Students accuse officials of corruption and neglecting education. They are also angry over plans to sell off pristine parts of Albania's southern coast for a luxury resort development backed by Jared Kushner, the son-in-law of US President Donald Trump. The resort plan has faced legal challenges.

The demonstrations follow months of mostly peaceful protests that the students say the government ignored. "We've been protesting peacefully for 140 days, and nothing was heard. So this is our only chance to be heard and get our rights that we deserve," a 19-year-old protester named Klea told Reuters.

Another 19-year-old protester named Enea had a message for Rama: "To resign as fast as possible before everything burns down."
