Police Foil Mass Shooting Plot at Illinois Hindu Temple

Arrested Before He Could Act

CHICAGO — Police in a Chicago suburb thwarted a potential mass shooting and arrested a 19-year-old man suspected of planning to attack a Hindu house of worship. Alexandros Paporakis, a Taco Bell cashier from Palatine, Illinois, was arrested on Sunday (11/10/2026) as he left his home — the same day investigators believe he planned to carry out an attack.

Paporakis faces felony charges of making a terrorist threat and unlawfully possessing weapons. He appeared in court on Wednesday (14/10/2026), and a Cook County judge ordered him held without bail.

The investigation began after Paporakis's Taco Bell co-workers reported receiving suspicious digital communications from him, including a document allegedly detailing a mass-shooting plan and photos of tactical gear. The Mount Prospect and Palatine police departments coordinated to locate and arrest Paporakis.

Manifesto and Seized Weapons

According to Cook County prosecutors, Paporakis sent a co-worker an eight-page document titled ‘Why I fought: A.K.A. (Aurora Shooter's Manifesto)’ describing Indian, Jewish, and African American people as ‘subhumans’ and outlining a plan to kill as many of them as possible, per court documents obtained by the Associated Press.

His co-workers said Paporakis openly identified as a white supremacist at work and said he could not wait to kill Indian people.

A search of Paporakis's home turned up an AR-style rifle without a serial number, loaded magazines, ammunition, a gas canister labeled with a slur targeting South Asians, and several items bearing Nazi symbolism, according to police and prosecutors.

Gun Jammed, Attack Failed

According to prosecutors, Paporakis drove to the Hindu temple in Aurora carrying a modified rifle and about 300 rounds of ammunition, equipped with body armor and other tactical gear. Authorities believe he intended to carry out a shooting but returned home after his rifle malfunctioned.

After arriving home, the suspect searched the internet for how to unjam the gun and allegedly called a colleague to inform them of his intent to carry out a mass shooting, according to police. Paporakis allegedly called a co-worker on Sunday morning saying his gun had jammed before leaving ‘super fast’ without being seen.

Palatine Police Chief William Nord stressed the seriousness of the threat. “The seriousness of this alleged threat cannot be overstated,” Nord said, adding that protecting houses of worship is a law enforcement priority.

US Lawmakers Condemn Plot

Several US lawmakers expressed shock at the alleged mass-shooting attempt at the Illinois Hindu temple. Senators Chuck Schumer, John Cornyn, and Jacky Rosen praised the swift police action that foiled the plot.

“Anti-Indian and anti-Hindu rhetoric is at an alarming level in America, and now we are seeing that hatred turn into violence,” said Schumer, the Democratic senator from New York, in a post on X.

The Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus (CAPAC) and the nonprofit ADL also voiced concern that houses of worship across faiths are becoming targets of hate and extremism. According to the HinduBusinessLine, the target was the Sri Venkateswara Temple in Aurora — the largest Hindu temple in the area.
