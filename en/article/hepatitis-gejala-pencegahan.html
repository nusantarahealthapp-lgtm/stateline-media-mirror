Hepatitis: Know the Symptoms and How to Prevent It

Stateline MEDIA Newsroom•October 14, 2026•3 min read

Hepatitis is liver inflammation caused by viruses A through E. Know the symptoms — fever, nausea, dark urine, yellow skin — and prevent it with hepatitis A and B vaccination plus clean living habits.

Key Facts

  • Hepatitis is liver inflammation; there are five hepatitis viruses: A, B, C, D, and E — according to WHO.
  • Hepatitis symptoms per WHO: fever, malaise, loss of appetite, diarrhoea, nausea, abdominal discomfort, dark-coloured urine, and yellowing of the skin and eyes; many patients show only mild symptoms or none at all.
  • Hepatitis B, C, and D viruses can cause chronic infection that develops into cirrhosis (liver scarring) or liver cancer.
  • Transmission: hepatitis A and E spread through contaminated food or water (faecal-oral route); hepatitis B, C, and D spread through blood and body fluids; hepatitis D only affects people already infected with hepatitis B.
  • Prevention per WHO: safe and effective vaccines exist for hepatitis B (the B vaccine also protects against hepatitis D) as well as vaccines for hepatitis A and E.
  • There is no vaccine for hepatitis C, but antiviral medicines can cure more than 95 percent of patients.

Getting to Know the Five Types of Hepatitis

Hepatitis is inflammation of the liver. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), there are five hepatitis viruses: A, B, C, D, and E. Each has different transmission routes and severity levels, but all attack the liver.

Hepatitis B, C, and D deserve special attention because they can cause chronic infection that over time develops into cirrhosis (liver scarring) or liver cancer. Chronically infected patients risk premature death if untreated.

Hepatitis Symptoms to Watch For

According to WHO, many people with hepatitis A, B, C, D, or E show only mild symptoms or no symptoms at all. Symptoms that may appear include fever, malaise, loss of appetite, diarrhoea, nausea, abdominal pain or discomfort, dark-coloured urine, and yellowing of the skin and whites of the eyes.

In acute hepatitis B, symptoms usually appear 1 to several months after infection: abdominal pain, dark urine, fever, joint pain, nausea and vomiting, and fatigue. Some people — especially children — may show no symptoms at all. If you experience these symptoms, consult a doctor or health facility for examination promptly.

How Each Type Spreads

Hepatitis A and E spread via the faecal-oral route — for example through food or drinking water contaminated with an infected person's stool. The virus therefore spreads easily in areas with poor sanitation or poor personal hygiene.

Hepatitis B, C, and D spread through blood and body fluids — for example via unsafe blood transfusions, shared needles, unprotected sex, and mother-to-child transmission during childbirth. Hepatitis D is only found in people already infected with hepatitis B, and the dual infection can accelerate liver damage.

Prevention: Vaccines and Clean Habits

The good news is that most hepatitis harm is preventable. According to WHO, safe and effective vaccines exist to prevent hepatitis B infection — when given at birth, the vaccine greatly reduces mother-to-child transmission risk. Hepatitis B vaccination also protects against hepatitis D infection. Vaccines also exist for hepatitis A and E.

For hepatitis A, practice clean habits: wash hands diligently with soap, eat well-cooked food, and drink clean water. Hepatitis C has no vaccine yet, but modern antiviral medicines can cure more than 95 percent of patients. Consult your doctor about the right vaccination and screening schedule for you and your family.
