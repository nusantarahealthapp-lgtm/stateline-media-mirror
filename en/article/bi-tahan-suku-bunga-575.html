BI Holds Rate at 5.75%, Economists: The Right Call

Stateline MEDIA Newsroom•October 14, 2026•2 min read

BI held its benchmark rate at 5.75% at the 22-23 September 2026 board meeting, the first chaired by Governor Destry Damayanti. Economists say the move rightly safeguards rupiah stability.

Key Facts

  • Bank Indonesia held the BI-Rate at 5.75%, the Deposit Facility rate at 4.75%, and the Lending Facility rate at 6.50% at its 22-23 September 2026 board meeting.
  • It was the first Board of Governors meeting chaired by BI Governor Destry Damayanti.
  • BCA chief economist David Sumual called the decision the right move to keep the rupiah stable without squeezing the real sector.
  • Permata Bank chief economist Josua Pardede described it as a tactical pause after BI raised rates by a cumulative 100 basis points in May-July 2026.
  • Bank lending grew 13.36% yoy in August 2026; the rupiah held steady around Rp17,500-Rp17,850.
  • BI is strengthening hedging incentives via swaps and DNDF, expanding local-currency transactions, and preparing the RPIM ratio effective 1 October 2026.

BI Holds Rate at 5.75%

Bank Indonesia (BI) decided to hold its benchmark interest rate, the BI-Rate, at 5.75%. The decision was taken at the Board of Governors meeting on 22-23 September 2026.

Alongside the BI-Rate, BI kept the Deposit Facility rate at 4.75% and the Lending Facility rate at 6.50%. It was the first board meeting chaired by BI Governor Destry Damayanti.

BI said the decision is consistent with its strategy of stabilizing the rupiah amid external pressures while preserving room for domestic economic growth.

Economists: Right Call, Tactical Pause

BCA chief economist David Sumual said the decision was the right step to maintain rupiah stability without adding pressure on the real sector. In his view, BI can still rely on hedging instruments to keep the rupiah steady.

Keeping the BI-Rate at 5.75% was judged effective in anchoring the rupiah, which held stable around Rp17,500-Rp17,850. A stable rupiah also dampens imported-goods inflation.

Meanwhile, Permata Bank chief economist Josua Pardede called the hold a tactical pause amid global financial market pressure, after the central bank raised rates by a cumulative 100 basis points in May-July 2026.

Stronger Hedging and Macroprudential Policy

On transmission, earlier rate hikes still left room for bank lending growth, which reached 13.36% year-on-year in August 2026.

BI is also strengthening hedging incentives through swap instruments and Domestic Non-Deliverable Forwards (DNDF), and expanding Local Currency Transaction (LCT) deals with partner countries.

The central bank kept macroprudential and payment-system policies accommodative, including preparations for the Macroprudential Inclusive Financing Ratio (RPIM) effective 1 October 2026 and the Indonesia Intermediation Acceleration Program (PINISI) to channel credit to productive sectors.

Josua believes BI should keep the BI-Rate at 5.75% as long as rupiah pressure can be managed through intervention, hedging incentives, and liquidity management. But BI should prepare a rate-hike option if global pressure persists and starts affecting the rupiah and inflation. BI also kept macroprudential policy accommodative to support productive lending growth.
